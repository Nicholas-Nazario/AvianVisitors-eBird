# Contributing artwork

Thank you for helping expand the AvianVisitors-eBird collection. Artwork is
submitted and reviewed through GitHub Issues so species, files, and attribution
stay together.

## Submission process

1. [Open a new artwork issue](https://github.com/Nicholas-Nazario/AvianVisitors-eBird/issues/new).
2. Add the `new artwork` label. This label is required for artwork tracking.
3. Attach the artwork file to the issue. PNG with a transparent background is
   preferred; include both a perched and an in-flight version when available.
4. Describe the species using its scientific name and common name.
5. Provide attribution information for the artist: the name to display and any
   preferred website or social links. If the work is based on or adapted from
   another source, include that source and its license or permission details.
6. Confirm that you created the work or have permission to submit it for use in
   this project. The project is licensed CC-BY-NC-SA-4.0, so contributions must
   be compatible with attribution and noncommercial sharing requirements.

Issues may be reviewed for species accuracy, consistency with the collection,
file quality, licensing, and attribution before artwork is added. A submission
may be edited or declined if it needs more information or does not fit the
project.

## Style guidelines

The collection uses a sparse Edo-period Japanese kachō-e woodblock-print
influence: confident sumi-e ink linework with soft watercolor washes and an
earthy, restrained palette of burnt umber, ochre, indigo, vermillion, and
muted greens.

- Use only a few broad, flat color zones with sharp boundaries. Abstract fine
  streaking, mottling, barring, and other subtle plumage into larger shapes.
- Keep the body simple and expressive, with a few confident outline strokes and
  only a small number of accent strokes for major wing or tail markings. Avoid
  feather-by-feather rendering, stippling, hatching, gradients, and photorealism.
- Make the target species recognizable. Pay attention to its diagnostic head,
  throat, wing, tail, bill, eye, leg, and plumage markings rather than relying
  on a generic bird silhouette.
- Keep the anatomy natural and the pose clear. Perched birds should have small,
  delicate feet; in-flight birds can tuck their feet or trail them naturally
  with the body.
- Fit the entire bird in frame with generous padding. The bird should occupy
  roughly one-third to one-half of the image, leaving generous negative space.
- Use a consistent warm cream paper-like ground for the collection. 
  Accepted artwork will have the background removed for rendering purposes
- Do not include branches, twigs, leaves, foliage, scenery, sky, water, borders,
  captions, signatures, seals, or watermarks. A perch may be implied by the
  posture but must not be drawn.

## Use of AI
- The original set of images for this site were generated by AI in order to cover a broad selection of species. 
- The long-term goal is to replace all the AI generated images with human created ones.
- In the atlas tab AI images will be marked as such. Artist created images will include details about the artist

## Reference material

These resources provide historical and visual context for Japanese bird-and-
flower prints and the kachō-e tradition:

- [Japanese Bird-and-Flower Prints](https://risdmuseum.org/exhibitions-events/exhibitions/japanese-bird-and-flower-prints) from the RISD Museum.
- [Transmission and Kachō-e: Birds, Flowers, and Tradition](https://diluo.digital.conncoll.edu/Asianart/exhibition/transmission-and-kacho-e-birds-flowers-and-tradition/) from the Digital Asian Art exhibition.

Artists to explore include the following. They worked across varied subjects,
so use these as references for composition, line, brushwork, and color rather
than as a requirement to copy any particular image:

- [Ohara Koson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohara_Koson), known especially
  for bird-and-flower prints.
- [Katsushika Hokusai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hokusai), whose broader
  body of work includes important examples of Japanese woodblock-print design.
- Kōno Bairei, especially *Album of One Hundred Birds*:
  - [Sainsbury Institute: Album of One Hundred Birds by Kōno Bairei](https://www.sainsbury-institute.org/library-treasures/album-of-one-hundred-birds-by-kono-bairei/)
  - [Metropolitan Museum of Art digital collection](https://libmma.contentdm.oclc.org/digital/collection/p16028coll7/id/31)
